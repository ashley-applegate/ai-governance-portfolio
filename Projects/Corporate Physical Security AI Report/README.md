# AI Governance in Corporate Physical Security

## Overview

For this project, I examined how artificial intelligence is changing corporate physical security operations, including surveillance, access control, incident response, and security personnel responsibilities.

Drawing on my professional background in physical security, I explored how AI could improve operational efficiency while introducing new challenges involving privacy, accuracy, accountability, and human oversight.

## Problem Statement

Corporate physical security teams are increasingly adopting technologies that automate monitoring, reporting, and incident detection.

While these tools can improve security operations, they also introduce risks involving sensitive data, inaccurate alerts, third-party vendors, and overreliance on automated decisions.

This project examines how organizations can approach AI adoption while balancing operational benefits with privacy protections and human oversight.

## Research Approach

I conducted a literature-based sector analysis covering:

- Historical development: The evolution of physical security technology, from traditional patrol systems to modern surveillance and access control.
- Current applications: Security robotics, automated monitoring, incident reporting, and integrated security systems.
- Future opportunities: AI-assisted pattern detection, predictive analysis, and changes to security personnel responsibilities.
- Risks and limitations: Privacy, data retention, algorithmic bias, false positives and negatives, cybersecurity vulnerabilities, and workforce impacts.
- Governance considerations: Human oversight, cross-functional collaboration, and responsible implementation of AI technologies.

## Key Findings

#### 1. AI could help security teams move from reactive to proactive operations

AI-assisted analysis of incident reports, surveillance footage, and alarm data could help organizations identify patterns and emerging risks that might otherwise be difficult to recognize.

These capabilities could support better resource allocation, risk identification, and operational planning.

#### 2. Automation creates opportunities to change security roles

AI-enabled monitoring and robotics could reduce repetitive tasks and allow security personnel to focus on more complex responsibilities.

However, successful adoption would require additional training, clear responsibilities, and continued human judgment when interpreting AI-generated information.

#### 3. Privacy and accuracy are significant concerns

Physical security systems collect sensitive information through surveillance, access control, and incident reporting.

Introducing AI raises questions about how this information is collected, retained, shared, and potentially used by third-party vendors.

Inaccurate alerts, biased outputs, or missed incidents could also create operational and safety risks.

#### 4. Human oversight remains essential

AI can support the identification of potential security threats, but automated findings still require appropriate review.

Organizations need to establish clear expectations for human involvement, particularly when decisions could affect an individual's safety, privacy, or access to a facility.

## Limitations

This was a research-based academic sector analysis, not an assessment of an organization's existing AI systems or security operations.

The report draws on published research and industry sources to examine potential applications, risks, and future developments. It does not include independent testing of AI-enabled security products.

## What I Learned

This project helped me connect my experience in corporate physical security with the governance considerations involved in adopting AI.

It reinforced the importance of involving privacy, legal, and security teams early in technology decisions rather than addressing potential risks after implementation.

I also explored how AI adoption could change the responsibilities of security personnel and why training and human oversight should remain part of the process.
