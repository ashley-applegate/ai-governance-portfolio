# Trust and Verification in Estate Administration

## Overview

For this project, I examined how Microsoft Copilot could support estate administration tasks and where its responses required human verification.

Using a Vermont probate administration scenario, I explored how AI could help organize information while also identifying inaccuracies and omissions that could have serious consequences.

## Problem Statement

AI can make complex administrative work easier, but inaccurate or incomplete information can create significant risks when legal obligations and deadlines are involved.

This project explores the benefits and limitations of using generative AI to support estate administration, particularly when individuals may not have prior experience navigating probate requirements.

## Evaluation Approach

I examined Copilot's responses across three areas:

- **Administrative support:** Organizing travel, mileage, and work records into a structured accounting table.
- **Legal information accuracy:** Summarizing administrator responsibilities using a Vermont probate guidance booklet.
- **Source verification:** Tracing an AI-generated prediction about efficiency improvements back to its cited source.

I compared the outputs with the information provided, identified discrepancies, and considered where human review was necessary.

## Key Findings

### 1. AI helped organize administrative records

Copilot converted a collection of dates, locations, and estate-related activities into a structured accounting table.

However, some driving-time estimates were unrealistically short, requiring correction before the information could be used.

### 2. Missing legal context created a significant risk

When summarizing administrator responsibilities, Copilot omitted information that changed the apparent deadline for submitting an estate inventory.

The distinction between 30 days from death and 30 days from court appointment was particularly important because relying on the wrong timeline could affect compliance with court requirements.

### 3. An efficiency claim did not match its original context

Copilot referenced a projected 30–50% reduction in drafting and administration time.

When I traced the claim to its source, I found that the figure concerned drafting work in estate-planning law firms, not the broader responsibilities of individuals administering a family member's estate.

This demonstrated how AI-generated summaries can make a claim appear more broadly applicable than its source supports.

## Limitations

This was an academic examination of selected AI interactions, not a comprehensive assessment of Copilot or a legal compliance review.

The findings reflect the specific tasks and responses examined. They do not establish how consistently Copilot would perform across other estate administration scenarios.

## What I Learned

This project showed me how AI can be helpful for organizing complicated administrative information while still producing errors that may be difficult to notice.

It also reinforced the importance of checking original sources, especially when an AI-generated answer involves legal deadlines, financial records, or claims that could influence someone's decisions.
